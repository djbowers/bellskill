## The standard

The get-up (Turkish get-up, TGU) moves from lying on the floor to standing —
and back — while a locked-out arm supports the bell overhead the entire time.
It is trained slowly and owned at every position; speed adds nothing.

## The positions, going up

1. **Roll to press.** Lying on your side curled around the bell, roll to your
   back and press the bell over the shoulder with both hands, then release the
   free hand. Eyes on the bell.
2. **Roll to elbow.** Drive through the bent-leg foot and roll diagonally onto
   the free elbow — a punch upward with the loaded arm, not a sit-up.
3. **Tall sit.** Straighten onto the free hand, chest proud, both shoulders
   packed away from the ears.
4. **Sweep to kneel.** Lift the hips (low sweep or high bridge), thread the
   straight leg back to a kneeling windmill position, then windshield-wipe to
   a lunge.
5. **Stand.** Look straight ahead from the lunge up. Lock the knees and stand
   fully tall before starting down.

Going down retraces the same positions in reverse, with the same control.

## Coaching cues

- The elbow of the loaded arm stays locked from floor to floor; a bent elbow
  ends the rep.
- Shoulder stays packed — the bell is held by the lats as much as the arm.
- Slow is the goal: a quality rep takes 30 seconds or more each way.
- Watch the bell until standing on the way up; on the way down, switch the
  eyes forward at the tall-sit as balance allows.

## Common faults

Rushing the roll-to-elbow into a crunch, letting the support-side shoulder
shrug, short-stepping the leg sweep so the knee lands under the body, and
standing up with a soft, unlocked elbow. Fix positions with a shoe balanced on
the fist or a light bell before loading heavier.
