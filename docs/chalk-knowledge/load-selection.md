## Starting sizes

Conventional starting bells for a healthy adult, adjusted for training
history:

- Men: 16 kg for most, 12 kg with no lifting background, 20-24 kg for an
  experienced barbell lifter learning the swing.
- Women: 8-12 kg to learn, with 12-16 kg arriving quickly for swings.

Grinds and ballistics diverge fast: most lifters swing one or two bell sizes
heavier than they press. It is normal to own a press bell, a swing bell, and
eventually a heavier hinge bell.

## When to move up

Move up one bell size (typically +4 kg) on a movement when all of these hold:

- Every prescribed set with the current bell is completed with crisp form and
  power to spare.
- The last rep of the last set looks like the first rep of the first set.
- RPE for the session sits comfortably below the ceiling — roughly 7 or less
  when the program does not say otherwise.

Introduce the new bell gradually: first sets of the session or one day a week,
letting it take over as it feels strong. Jumping every set to the new size at
once is the classic way to break technique.

## When to move down

Drop a size when form degrades mid-set, when returning from a layoff of more
than a couple of weeks, when learning a new movement pattern, or for
high-volume conditioning days where power per rep matters more than load.

## Two-bell (double) work

Doubles roughly double the load and dramatically increase core and leg demand.
A sensible entry to double work is a pair one size below the single-bell
working weight for that movement — e.g. comfortable pressing 24 kg means
starting double presses with 2×20 kg.
