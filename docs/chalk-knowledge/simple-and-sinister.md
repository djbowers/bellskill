## What it is

Simple & Sinister (S&S) is Pavel Tsatsouline's minimalist daily practice built
on exactly two movements: the one-hand swing and the get-up. A session is 100
one-hand swings (10 sets of 10, switching hands between sets) followed by 10
get-ups (5 per side, one rep at a time). It is designed to be trained
near-daily — five to six days a week — as a practice, not a workout to failure.

## Session structure

- Warm-up: goblet squats, halos, and bridges are the canonical prep.
- Swings: 10 sets of 10 one-hand swings. Rest between sets should keep the
  breath mostly nasal and the next set crisp; power on every rep matters more
  than compressing rest.
- Get-ups: 10 total reps (5 left, 5 right), performed slowly and deliberately
  with a pause at each position.

## Standards and progression

The "Simple" standard is 100 one-hand swings in 5 minutes and 10 get-ups in
10 minutes, done with 32 kg for men and 24 kg for women. Until the standard is
comfortable, sessions are untimed ("timeless") — the timed test is an
occasional check, not the daily practice.

Progression is by weight, not reps: when all sets with the current bell feel
strong and crisp, introduce the next bell size for a couple of sets and let it
gradually take over. A common path for men is 16 → 24 → 32 kg on swings, with
the get-up usually one bell behind the swing. The "Sinister" goal — 48 kg for
men, 32 kg for women — is a long-term aspiration, not a program requirement.

## When to recommend it

S&S suits a lifter who wants a simple daily default, is short on time (30
minutes), owns only one or two bells, or needs to rebuild consistency after a
layoff. It pairs a heavy hinge pattern with loaded mobility, so it leaves room
for pressing or squatting work on top if the lifter wants more.
