## What it is

The Rite of Passage (ROP), from Pavel Tsatsouline's Enter the Kettlebell, is a
classic press-focused program built on clean & press ladders three days a week
plus ballistic work (swings or snatches) on the same days. Its goal is a big
one-arm military press — the traditional target is pressing a bell close to
half your bodyweight.

## Ladder structure

A ladder is a series of rungs with increasing reps: clean once, press 1, rest
briefly; clean again, press 2; then press 3. That is one ladder of (1, 2, 3).
The lifter completes 3 to 5 ladders per session, alternating arms each rung.

- Heavy day: 5 ladders, working up to 5 rungs (1-2-3-4-5) over the weeks.
- Medium day: 4 ladders, one rung fewer than the heavy day.
- Light day: 3 ladders, two rungs fewer than the heavy day.

Progression: when all 5 heavy-day ladders of (1-2-3-4-5) are completed —
75 presses per arm across the session — move up to the next bell and restart
the ladder count.

## Ballistic work

After the ladders, roll a die (or pick): swings or snatches for 2 to 12
minutes, done in repeats with enough rest to keep every rep powerful. Variety
days emphasize volume on the light day and intensity on the heavy day.

## When to recommend it

ROP fits a lifter whose goal is pressing strength, who can commit to three
structured days a week, and who owns at least two bell sizes (a press bell and
a lighter ballistic bell helps). It is more demanding on the shoulders than
S&S; a lifter with cranky shoulders should own a clean pain-free press before
starting.
