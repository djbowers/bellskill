## The idea

Strong Endurance is StrongFirst's family of anti-glycolytic conditioning
protocols. Instead of grinding through burning, gasping sets (glycolytic
training), the work is organized as short, explosive repeats powered by the
alactic system, with enough rest for the aerobic system to recharge it. The
lifter trains fast and stops fresh, accumulating quality volume without the
acid bath.

## What a session looks like

The canonical shape is short repeats on a clock: for example, 5 explosive
snatches or 10 hard swings at the top of every 30 seconds to every minute,
repeated for 10 to 30+ sets. Every rep should be near-maximal power; the set
ends long before form or speed degrades. Heart rate drifts up slowly across
the session rather than spiking.

Two signals tell you the protocol is being respected: the breath recovers
between repeats (talk-test easy by the next set), and the last set is as crisp
as the first. If reps slow down or grip fails, the session has drifted
glycolytic — reduce the reps per repeat or extend the rest.

## Why it matters

- Power endurance improves without dreading the session, so frequency stays
  high.
- It is easy on recovery: most lifters can do anti-glycolytic repeats several
  days a week alongside strength work.
- Glycolytic "burner" sessions still have a place, but as an occasional test
  or peak — not the default conditioning diet.

## When to recommend it

Strong Endurance-style repeats suit a lifter whose goal is conditioning or
snatch-test prep, who tends to turn every session into a smoker, or who needs
conditioning that does not interfere with strength days.
