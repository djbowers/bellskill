## The standard

A hardstyle swing is a hip hinge, not a squat and not a front raise. The bell
is projected forward by a violent hip snap; the arms are ropes. At the top the
body forms a standing plank — glutes locked, quads locked, ribs down — with
the bell floating to roughly chest height. The back stays neutral throughout,
and the bell passes above the knees on the backswing.

## Common faults

- **Squatting the swing.** Knees shoot forward and the hips drop instead of
  hinging back. Fix: practice the hip hinge against a wall — push the hips
  back until the butt touches, shins near vertical.
- **Bell dipping below the knees.** The backswing gets low and the spine
  rounds under load. Fix: play "attack the zipper" — the forearms should
  contact high on the inner thighs, bell close to the groin.
- **Lifting with the arms.** The bell is muscled up to height instead of
  floating. Fix: think "jump through the bell"; let the hips set the height
  and accept a lower float if needed.
- **Leaning back at the top.** Hyperextending the lower back to finish. Fix:
  finish tall — squeeze the glutes and brace the abs like taking a punch.
- **No float / rushing the descent.** Catching the bell early and dropping
  into the hinge before the bell arrives. Fix: stay tall until the upper arms
  reconnect with the ribs, then hinge.
- **Losing power late in sets.** Reps get slow and quiet. Fix: end the set —
  every hardstyle rep should crack like a whip. Shorter, sharper sets beat
  long, mushy ones.

## Quick checks

Ballistic breath (sharp exhale at the top), a floating bell, and soles planted
flat through the whole rep. If any of those disappear, address the fault
before adding weight or volume.
