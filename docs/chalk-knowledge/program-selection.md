## Match the program to the goal

- **General strength and conditioning with minimal time:** a daily-practice
  hinge + get-up program (Simple & Sinister pattern) is the default
  recommendation. Two movements, one bell, ~30 minutes.
- **Pressing strength:** a ladder-based clean & press program (Rite of Passage
  pattern) three days a week, with ballistic work attached.
- **Conditioning, fat loss, or snatch-test prep:** anti-glycolytic repeat
  protocols (Strong Endurance pattern) — short explosive sets on a clock,
  stopping fresh.
- **Muscle and work capacity:** complexes and chains (e.g. clean + press +
  squat done back-to-back without setting the bell down) or double-bell
  programs, if the lifter owns a matched pair.

## Signals from training history

- Long gaps between sessions favor a simple, near-daily practice over a rigid
  three-day split — consistency is the limiting factor, not programming.
- A movement mix living entirely on grinds suggests adding a ballistic
  program block; all-ballistic history suggests a strength block.
- A pattern that is chronically overdue (hinge, squat, press, pull, carry)
  should steer program choice toward one that trains it on most days.

## Rules of thumb

- Run one program at a time, for its intended duration, before judging it.
- Program-hopping every two weeks is the most common progress killer; the
  second most common is adding "extra credit" volume on top of a program that
  is already sufficient.
- A deload — lighter or shorter week — roughly every 4-6 weeks of hard
  progression keeps joints and enthusiasm intact.
