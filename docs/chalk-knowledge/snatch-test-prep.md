## The standard

The classic kettlebell snatch test is 100 snatches in 5 minutes, switching
hands as needed, without setting the bell down. The traditional test sizes are
24 kg for men and 16 kg for women (lighter divisions exist by bodyweight and
age in certification contexts). It is a benchmark of power endurance, not a
training staple to be tested weekly.

## Prerequisites

Before chasing the test, a lifter should own a technically sound snatch — a
one-arm swing that finishes overhead in one uninterrupted arc, with a soft
"tame the arc" catch and no banging on the forearm — and be comfortable with
high-volume one-hand swing sessions at the test weight.

## How to prepare

- **Build the engine anti-glycolytically.** Most prep volume is short, crisp
  repeats — 5 to 10 snatches on the minute or every 30 seconds — accumulating
  60-200 quality reps per session while staying fresh (see Strong Endurance
  basics).
- **Practice pacing.** 100 reps in 5 minutes is 20 reps per minute. Common
  strategies: steady sets of 10 per hand switching each minute, or front-load
  larger sets in the first two minutes while the grip is fresh.
- **Protect the grip and the hands.** Grip is the usual limiter. Rotate in
  swing volume, keep calluses shaved, and stop a training set before the grip
  fails rather than tearing.
- **Test rarely.** A full-out 5-minute test is highly glycolytic; run it every
  several weeks at most, with a deload before.

## Red flags

Ripped hands, a snatch that descends in two stages (drop to the shoulder,
then swing), or reps that grind past the sticking point all mean the lifter
should go back to technique and lighter repeats before pushing test pacing.
